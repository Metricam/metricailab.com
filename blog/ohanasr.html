Introducing OhanASR v0.1

State-of-the-art open speech recognition for Armenian — one model for recorded audio, one for live transcription.

Overview

Today we're releasing OhanASR, a family of open-weight speech recognition models for Armenian. The flagship, OhanASR-v0.1, is the strongest open Armenian ASR model available: on ArmBench-ASR it ranks first by character error rate and second by word error rate across every system we evaluated — open or closed.

The family is named after Dzenov Ohan — Ohan the Loud-Voiced — from the Armenian epic Daredevils of Sassoun, whose shout could be heard across the mountains. It felt right for models whose whole job is listening to Armenian.

Armenian is spoken by millions of people, yet it has long been an afterthought for speech technology: large multilingual systems treat it as one of a hundred languages, and the open options have lagged far behind commercial APIs. OhanASR closes that gap and puts a state-of-the-art model in everyone's hands.

Two models, one family

We start from a 2-billion-parameter multilingual speech model and fine-tune it on 820 hours of Armenian speech spanning formal speech, everyday conversation and literary readings. From there the family splits in two:

  • OhanASR-v0.1 — the accuracy-first model for recorded audio: interviews, lectures, podcasts, archives, call recordings.
  • OhanASR-Streaming-v0.1 — continued training on top of OhanASR-v0.1 so it can emit text incrementally as audio arrives. Built for live captions, voice agents and anything that can't wait for the speaker to finish.

Results

We evaluate on ArmBench-ASR, our open benchmark for Armenian speech recognition. It pools five very different kinds of audio — crowd-sourced read speech (Common Voice 26), FLEURS, recited poetry, film dialogue and broadcast news — so a model can't win by being good at just one register.

The streaming model gives up about three points of WER in exchange for real-time output — a modest price, and it still places in the top ten overall, ahead of many systems that only work offline.

By domain

Read speech and news are close to solved: on FLEURS, OhanASR-v0.1 gets fewer than one character in a hundred wrong after normalization. Poetry is harder on raw WER — mostly punctuation and archaic spellings, which normalization largely forgives. Film dialogue, with overlapping voices, music and slang, remains the real frontier.

Known limitations

This is a v0.1, and we'd rather you hear about the rough edges from us:

  • Formal vs. colloquial Armenian. The models sometimes mix registers — for example writing «ա» where the formal form is «է».
  • Code-switching. Speech that mixes Armenian with English or Russian isn't always handled well; foreign words may come out transliterated into Armenian script.
  • Unclear audio. In noisy or mumbled segments the models may drop words they can't resolve rather than guess.

What's next

We're already training the next version, focused on three things: higher accuracy — especially on conversational and film audio — lower streaming latency, and robustness to noise, accents and code-switching. OhanASR is also one half of a voice stack: pair it with OhanTTS for speech generation and Ayb for language understanding.

Try it, break it, and tell us what you find — feedback is how v0.2 gets better.
