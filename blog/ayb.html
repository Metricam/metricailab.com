Overview

Today we're releasing Ayb v0.5, our family of open large language models for Armenian and our first step towards sovereign Armenian AI. Its largest version, Ayb 9B, ranks #3 of all models on ArmBench, ahead of GPT-5.2 Pro, Gemini 3 Flash and Claude 3.7 Sonnet. The only open model above it is the 763B DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash.

Ayb was trained end to end, from continued pretraining to reinforcement learning, with a tokenizer extended for Armenian. That makes it much cheaper and faster to run on Armenian text than closed models such as Claude. It understands images as well as text, and comes in three sizes: 0.8B, 4B and 9B.

Այբ (Ayb) is the first letter of the Armenian alphabet, created by Mesrop Mashtots in 405 AD. Written Armenian begins with it, and so does our Armenian AI stack.

Sovereign Armenian AI

Until now, using a top-tier language model in Armenian meant sending your data to a foreign API, paying per token for a language those models were never optimized for, and depending on decisions made somewhere else. For governments, banks, hospitals and schools that is often not an option at all.

Ayb changes that. The weights are open, so the model can run on your own infrastructure, in Armenia, be audited, and be fine-tuned on your own data, without giving up quality. Ayb v0.5 is the first release on that path. Together with OhanASR and OhanTTS, it covers listening, reasoning and speaking in Armenian on open models.

Three sizes

All three sizes are in one Hugging Face collection, so you can pick the balance of quality and cost that fits your use case:

  • Ayb 9B is the flagship: #3 on ArmBench, ahead of much larger open and proprietary models. It handles multi-turn conversations, complex instructions and tool calling.
  • Ayb 4B balances quality and compute (#8 on ArmBench), a practical choice for everyday Armenian applications and assistants.
  • Ayb 0.8B is built for constrained hardware and high-volume pipelines, including as the language component in speech recognition, text-to-speech and OCR systems. It still ranks #16 on ArmBench, ahead of Mistral Large and Gemini 2.5 Pro.

Trained end to end

Ayb is built on the Qwen3.5 architecture and adapted to Armenian through a full training pipeline:

  1. Armenian tokenizer. We extended the tokenizer with Armenian vocabulary while keeping the model's existing capabilities (more below).
  2. Continued pretraining on Armenian and English text, to deepen the model's Armenian vocabulary, grammar and knowledge. The corpus runs through mid-2026.
  3. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on instruction data, to follow Armenian requests, hold conversations and produce structured outputs.
  4. Post-training with DPO and GRPO, to align answers with what Armenian speakers prefer and sharpen accuracy.

An Armenian tokenizer

Most LLMs tokenize Armenian badly. Their vocabularies were built mostly for English, so Armenian words get split into many small pieces. Every extra token costs money, adds latency and uses up context.

We extended Ayb's tokenizer with Armenian-specific vocabulary. The same Armenian text takes far fewer tokens, so Ayb is significantly cheaper and faster to run than closed models such as Claude, and fits more Armenian into the same context window.

Results

We evaluated Ayb v0.5 zero-shot on two Armenian benchmarks. ArmBench, the open benchmark we built and maintain, covers 24 Armenian tasks across knowledge, reading comprehension, analysis, generation and correction. Armenian IFEval is the Armenian adaptation of Instruction-Following Evaluation.

ArmBench

Ayb 9B scores 0.6164 and ranks #3 of all models, open or closed, behind only GPT-6.1 Sol and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash. It beats GPT-5.2 Pro, Gemini 3 Flash, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Gemini 3 Pro and Claude Sonnet 4. Ayb 4B (0.5828) ranks #8, ahead of Grok 4 Fast and Gemini 2.5 Flash, and even Ayb 0.8B (0.5300) ranks #16.

All three models are strongest at Armenian reading comprehension, text generation and correction. Factual knowledge, complex reasoning and instruction following are where we see the most room to grow.

Armenian IFEval

On Armenian IFEval, Ayb 9B scores 0.729 (#16). That result comes with thinking completely disabled: Ayb has had no dedicated reasoning training yet, while almost every model around it on the leaderboard was run with some reasoning effort. Even so, it edges out its Qwen3.5 9B base running with thinking on.

Careful with medical advice

Ayb had no dedicated safety tuning, but it already shows safety-aware behavior, such as declining to prescribe medication.

Vision

Ayb keeps Qwen3.5's full vision-language architecture. We adapted only the language model, training it on Armenian and English text, and left the vision encoder and the vision-to-language projector unchanged. That keeps the base model's visual skills while letting Ayb describe images, answer questions about visual details and follow image-based instructions in Armenian.

Limitations

  • Trained mostly on Eastern Armenian. Western Armenian, regional dialects and Armenian in Latin script are less reliable.
  • Like any LLM, Ayb can state incorrect details, and its coverage of recent events is uneven.
  • Multi-step reasoning in thinking mode is still limited. All reported results use thinking disabled.
  • Preference tuning is still early, especially when several answers are correct but differ in clarity or usefulness.
Better together. Put OhanASR in front of Ayb and OhanTTS behind it, and you have an open, end-to-end Armenian voice assistant.

What's next

Ayb v0.5 is the first step. Next we're working on stronger multi-step reasoning, with Armenian-specific training and evaluation in thinking mode; better preference tuning, built on Armenian response comparisons that reward accuracy, clarity and following instructions; better support for Western Armenian and other variants; and broader evaluation on real-world applications.

We acknowledge the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking for awarding this project access to the EuroHPC supercomputer LUMI, hosted by CSC (Finland) and the LUMI consortium. Ayb 4B and Ayb 0.8B were trained entirely on LUMI.
